# Engagement

The retention layer. A clean ad that nobody watches past second two is a failure. This module makes the
ad *hold* — it governs the hook, the cut rhythm that drives the anchor/pacing plan, and hook-variant
testing. Apply it across stages 2, 7, and 10; it never overrides the Constitution or the claim flags.

**Out of scope (the user's CapCut craft, not the skill's):** captions, audio/music, SFX, and the exact
edit timing. The skill does not prescribe these — it plans and generates the footage; the final edit is
done by hand.

## 1. The hook (first 1–1.5 seconds) — the only second that matters

Most viewers decide to keep watching in the first ~1.5s. Engineer the opening for that, not for story.

- **The first frame is a stop-scroll, not a warm-up.** It must show the most absurd / curious / high-
  contrast image of the whole ad — the payoff of the visual joke, not the setup. If the opening frame
  is calm or generic, the ad is dead on arrival.
- **Open a loop in the first line.** The VO's first sentence should raise a question the viewer needs
  answered. Visual + VO both create the itch.
- **No logos, no slow intros, no brand bumper** in the first 2s — nothing that reads as "an ad."
- **Motion or an expressive face in frame one.** A living character beats an empty environment.

## 2. Retention cut-rhythm (drives the anchor + pacing plan)

This shapes how many anchors/animations to plan and how they pace — a generation concern, even though
the final cut happens in the edit.

- **Plan a visual change every ~1.5–2.5s.** New anchor, a punch-zoom, or a strong motion beat — the
  frame must keep changing or attention drops. This is why anchors get animated *and* cut, not held.
- **Front-load energy.** The biggest visual move belongs in the first shot, not saved for the end.
- **Speed up into the CTA.** Plan the last ~20% as the fastest-changing stretch for urgency; give the
  payoff frame one beat to land.
- **The spectacle shot** (the transformation) earns the most motion and screen time.

## 3. Hook-variant testing (the biggest free multiplier)

The same ad lives or dies on its opening. Always produce **3 different hooks** for the same body.

- Vary **the first 1–2s only**: a different opening visual gag, or a different opening VO line, over the
  identical rest of the ad.
- Ship all three; keep the winner by retention/CTR; reuse that hook shape next time.
- Cheaper than making three ads and outperforms polishing one.

## Engagement QC (before delivery)

- Does the **first frame** stop the scroll on its own, with sound off?
- Is there a **visual change at least every ~2.5s**?
- Is the **CTA** unmistakable at the end (faster cuts + the payoff beat)?
- Do you have **3 hook variants** to test?
- Sound **off** test: does the ad still read from the visuals alone?
